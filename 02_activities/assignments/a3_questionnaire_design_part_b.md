2. Given the class content (error, survey quality, ethics) covered since Part A of the assignment, do you wish to make any changes to your original survey? If so, make a copy of your original survey and make any desired changes. Describe the changes and your reasoning. Provide a link here for submission.

    ```
    Changes to my previous survey:
    1. What is your province of voting? (options: select one province/territory from a list which will be encoded on values 1-12)
    I changed the question to remove of residence because rather finding out where observational units are voting is more important than where they actually live. For the large majority of units, they will likely live in the same province as they vote.
    2. What is your identified gender? (options: male, female, non-binary)
    I added some options to the question to prevent users from writing in answers and restrict to specified categories. This may help prevent selection bias, such as undercoverage. 
    3. What is your identified ethnicity? (options: Oceania origins, North American Indigenous origins, European origins, Latin/Central/South American origins, Carribean origins, Other North American origins, African origins, Asian origins, select as many that apply)
    I used the same ethnic groups that were identified in this article by Stats Can from the 2016 census https://www12.statcan.gc.ca/census-recensement/2016/as-sa/98-200-x/2016016/98-200-x2016016-eng.cfm. This is to ensure we get a coverage of all available ethnicities within Canada and help prevent selection bias, such as undercoverage. 
    4. What is your age? (options: 18-24, 25-34, 35-44, 45-54, 55-64, 65-74, 75-84, 85-94, 95+, select one)
    This will ensure we capture the age distribution in which our voters belong too. The question itself did not change, only the option of answers. By having a select option, this will hopefully reduce processing errors such as typos.
    5. What is your approximate yearly income? (options: $1 to $35,808, $35,809 to $59,521, $59,522 to $88,658, $88,659 to $133,468, $133,469+.
    These are quintiles from Stats Canada https://publications.gc.ca/collections/collection_2021/schl-cmhc/nh70-1/NH70-1-1-2021-eng.pdf. Similar to the age question, providing available cohorts for selection prevents processing errors such as typo. This may also help prevent non-response errors where individuals may not want to provide their actual income. By having a range of incomes, I would hope observational units would correctly identify their quintile.
    6. What is your primary voter issue in the upcoming election and why? i.e. environment because I want better green energy initiatives.
    New Question: In a single sentence, please describe your primary voting issue in the upcoming election? i.e. I want to see improvements to green energy initiatives.
    I revised this question to be more specific to what I would desire as a survey administrator. My describing a length, it can prevent answers from becoming too long and make data processing easier. I also removed the why as it my appear as a double-barrelled questions where respondents may have to defend their issue. Rather, in the new question, respondents can just describe what their voting issue is without a need for rationale.
    7.  What is one current government policy, bill, program or initiative that could be improved?  
    I will remove this question. It appears quite similar to the previous question and I do not want there to be survey resondent burden. Having too many questions, especially if they are repetitive may lead to some respondents not completing this survey. Here I can prevent non-respondent bias.
    8.  Can you rank in order, the top 3 federal political parties you would vote for? (1 = most likely to vote for of the top 3, 3 = least likely to vote for of the top 3)
    New Question: What federal political party are you most likely to vote for? (Animal Protection Party, Bloc Quebecois, Canadian Future, Centrist Party, Christian Heritage Party, Communist Party, Conservative Party, Free Party, Green Party, Liberal Party, Libertarian Party, Marijuana Party, Marxist-Lenninist Party, Maverick Party, New Democratic Party, Parti Rhinoceros, People's Party, United Party, select one)
    I changed this question because realistically, most voters only care about the party they will vote for and the party they do not want to win. I decided to shorten the question to the most favourable party as it would be easier and quicker for respondents to complete. This will hopefully reduce non-respondent bias. I do think there are too many options above which allows for a reduction in coverage error, but may lead to a tradeoff in response bias because individuals may not read all options. In this tradeoff, I could probably reduce the options to the top 5 most popular choices that have consistently performed well in Canadian politics (Liberal, Conservative, NDP, Green, Bloc).
    9. What is one aspect you dislike about the oopposing political party?
    New Question: In a single sentence, please describe one aspect you dislike about the current political party in power?
    I changed this question because the original was confusing and difficult to interpret. There is no definition of the oppopsing political party and may be interpreted differently from many people. Rather, I specified to the current party in power which has its benefits. If the party I am working for is in power, it allows us to receive feedback on qualities we can improve and hopefully retain or gain voters for. If this is another political party, these qualities can serve as the foundation for attack ads and debate topics on television. I would hope the open-ended question allows for a multitude of answers. So despite the increased risk of data processing errors, the tradeoff of obtaining valuable information may be worth it.
    ```

3. Could the purpose of your survey have been fulfilled through an observational study? If yes, how? If no, why not?

    ```
    No, I do not believe my survey could have been fulfilled through an observational study. Theoretically, we could find a lot of the demographic information through observations. However, travelling throughout the country to suffice these stratifications would be extremely expensive, time consuming and inefficient. Questions 6, 7, 8 and 9 (now reduced to 3 questions) would require direct questioning of observational units. I would not be able to determine answers to political voting beliefs through observing someone's activities. While the argument stands you could scrape social media sites for an estimate, I think this can be problematic. A huge sampling bias exists where there is undercoverage. It is likely people who want to be outspoken, may share their political beliefs on social media, leaving a possible silent majority unaccounted for. Through observational studies, we would not capture the sample of votes who keep opinions to themselves and thus leading to a sampling bias through undercoverage and thus potential measurement error. 
    ```
